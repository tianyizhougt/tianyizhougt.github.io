I am a postdoctoral research scientist at Columbia University's Data Science Institute, working with [Samory Kpotufe](https://www.columbia.edu/~skk2175/) (Department of Statistics) and [Qiang Du](https://www.apam.columbia.edu/faculty/qiang-du) (Department of Applied Physics and Applied Mathematics). 

I develop statistical foundations and learning algorithms for reliable AI systems, with cybersecurity as a central motivation. My research asks how AI systems can detect evolving cyber-attacks when yesterday’s data no longer reflect today’s threats. My work develops principled ways to use synthetic data for anomaly detection and to transfer knowledge across sequentially arriving tasks. I also study how neural network architectures and representations of functional data shape learning and generalization. My broader goal is to enable AI systems to improve through experience while recognizing when past knowledge has become outdated.

I received my Ph.D. in Machine Learning from Georgia Institute of Technology [ISyE](https://www.isye.gatech.edu/), where I was fortunate to be advised by [Xiaoming Huo](https://www.isye.gatech.edu/users/xiaoming-huo). Originally from Hong Kong, I completed my B.Sc. in Statistics with first-class honors at the University of Hong Kong in 2020. During my undergraduate studies, I had the opportunity to study mathematics and statistics as an exchange student at UCLA in 2019.
